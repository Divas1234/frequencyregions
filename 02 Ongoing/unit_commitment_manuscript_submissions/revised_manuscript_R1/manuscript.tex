\begin{abstract}

	Frequency dynamic fills with uncertain-variability features due to the increasing penetration of converter-interfaced generators, where this feature trends to be bottlenecks in selecting scheduled commitments solved by Frequency-Constrained Unit Commitment~(FCUC). To address this, a Bayesian-filter-derived System-Frequency-Response~(BF-SFR) model is proposed and a refined FCUC (r-FCUC) is then extended. Significantly, the uncertain-variability feature due to the intermittent output of converter-interfaced generators, measured residuals, etc., is retreated as a chronological stochastic process. As such, the frequency dynamic can be extracted as a sequential deterministic process within a Bayesian-filter framework. In contrast to existing methodologies such as analytical SFR or time-discrete SFR methods, the proposed BF-SFR method can capture the trajectory of frequency dynamics in the presence of uncertain-variability features and estimate its posterior distribution. Furthermore, the frequency dynamic through BF-SFR is reshaped as a group of chance-constrained frequency dynamic constraints and subsequently integrated into r-FCUC. Because the reformulated frequency constraints in r-FCUC account for potential uncertain-variability features, the resulting scheduled commitment are well suited to meet actual frequency requirements. Several results on a modified IEEE 6 bus test system and IEEE 118 bus test system are implemented to check the validity of the proposed methods.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{I}{ncreasing} penetration of renewable energy challenges the security of power systems under complex disturbance cases. In such situations, independent system operators (ISO) must set aside sufficient resources to guarantee the security of scheduled commitments, \cite{9613814}. Nevertheless, since frequency dynamic processes has significant uncertain-variability features along with non-fluctuating controllable generators replaced by converter-interfaced resources (wind farms and solar stations), it is hard to maintain the security of scheduled commitments without considering uncertain-variability features. Therefore, developing a reliable SFR-constructed method and associated FCUC model is paramount to capture the uncertain-variability feature of frequency dynamics.

Existing literature reported an amount number of analytical models and time-discretized models to model frequency dynamics. The analytical models reflect the average system frequency changing among all synchronize generators. It posits that those generators with similar control characteristics will maintain common frequency dynamics. Consequently, the generators participating into SFR can be aggregated into a limited number of aggregated units, which simplifies the swing function as an ordinary differential equation (ODE) and prevents non-analytical solutions. Ref.~\cite{anderson1990low} proposed a second-order ODE to represent frequency dynamics in the systems dominated by conventional generators. Ref.~\cite{shi2018analytical,huang2025wind} introduced a refined SFR method for identifying frequency parameters in low-inertia grids and extended the analytical SFR model. Ref.~\cite{shen2020frequency} integrated the linearized control schemes of converter-interfaced reources into frequency dynamics. Ref.~\cite{paturet2020stochastic,9712362,zhang2020modeling,markovic2018lqr,ding2021two} extended a corrected SFR model, where the benefit of wind farms with VI control and droop control is incorporated. Furthermore, Ref.~\cite{9765359} constructed an advanced SFR model that considers the impact of variable-droop control characteristic of wind farms. These analytical models yield similar results, with the impact of heterogeneous generators on frequency dynamics primarily dependent on the selection of several frequency parameters. Another analytical model, known as average system frequency (ASF),~\cite{garcia2021requirements,9449643}, assumes that different generators have specific control characteristics while maintaining consistent frequency dynamics, as discussed in Ref.~\cite{garcia2021requirements,9449643}. While potentially more accurate in representing system behavior, these methods often sacrifice analytical tractability, requiring numerical solutions.

Conversely, the discretized type of frequency dynamic model offers greater flexibility regarding multi-machine frequency dynamic modeling and various control characteristics analysis. These models utilize interpolation methods, such as Euler method and Runge-Kutta method, to solve the differential equations of frequency dynamics. Ref.~\cite{10219063} assumed that reheated generators share similar time-delay characteristics and derived a discretized solution for frequency dynamics. Ref.~\cite{vatani2015relay} considered different time-relay characteristics of converters and developed a corrected discretized method. Ref.~\cite{oskouee2021primary,mahmoudnia2024enhancing} incorporated the droop control characteristic from flywheel storage into frequency dynamics, and constructed a revised discretized solution. Ref.~\cite{ye2015analytical} comprehensively accounted for the effects of converter-interfaced generators alongside conventional generators and rebuilt a discretized frequency dynamic model. These discretized model highlight the evolution of frequency dynamics while considering various control schemes. Nevertheless, discretized models require more computing resource in comparison with SFR and ASF. While analytical models excel at analyzing the dynamic evolution of frequency, discretized methods are more effective for investigating the influence of specific control parameters on frequency dynamic processes. In addition, the uncertain-variability feature of converter-interfaced generators participating in frequency support has not yet been thoroughly examined.

Uncertain-variability feature, caused by converter-interfaced generators and measured residuals, is becoming a significant factor affecting frequency dynamic securities, and this issue is pronounced in the low-inertia grid. As conventional generators become less capable of providing sufficient frequency reserves, the system becomes increasingly reliant on converter-interfaced resources, making it more susceptible to the adverse effects of their inherent uncertain variability features on frequency dynamics. Ref.~\cite{8478399,8122022} analyzed frequency dynamics by considering uncertain-variability features as a \(\textit{It}\widehat{o}\) process, where uncertain-variability features are retreated to be a prior sequential random sequence following Gaussian distribution. Due to excellent conjugate properties of Gaussian distribution families, the frequency dynamics derived from the \(\textit{It}\widehat{o}\) process result in an analytical solution. However, these uncertain-variability factors are inherently dependent on real-time frequency variations in actual engineering processes, making their posterior distribution conditional and unpredictable. Consequently, existing frequency dynamic models that address uncertain-variability, in \cite{8478399,8122022}, may not accurately capture the true posterior of frequency dynamics.

By constructing adaptive frequency constraints and embedding them into UC, FCUC have successfully demonstrated their effectiveness, \cite{10098683,6269098,9765359,restrepo2005unit,zhang2020modeling,brito2018unit,6269098,7822944,9964327,liu2019model,10384867,wei2025data,10820061}, in ensuring frequency security by incorporating constraints related to RoCoF, frequency nadir, quasi-steady-state frequency deviation (Qss), frequency containment reserve (FCR), and frequency replacement reserve (FRR), etc. These constraints are typically formulated as linear or nonlinear functions. For instance, Ref.\cite{oskouee2021primary,mahmoudnia2024enhancing} constructed a set of nonlinear RoCoF, nadir, and Qss constraints derived from analytical SFR model and developed a FCUC model. Ref.~\cite{paturet2020stochastic,9765359} constructed a max-affine-derived linearized frequency nadir constraint and extended a refined FCUC. Ref.~\cite{10098683,7822944,9964327,liu2019model,10384867,wei2025data,10820061} applied various neural networks to learn mapping relations between frequency nadir and dynamic parameters. However, a limitation of these approaches is that the resulting frequency constraints within previous FCUC models mentioned before are deterministic, thus implicitly neglecting the inherent uncertain-variability features present in frequency dynamic processes.

Motivated by these limitations mentioned before, this study proposes a novel SFR method, termed BF-SFR, designed to explicitly incorporate uncertain-variability features. These features can be forecasted as stochastic waveform signals captured by measurement devices, enabling their posterior distributions to be observed and integrated into the calculation of frequency dynamics. Consequently, the trajectory of frequency dynamics is represented as a sequential stochastic process, solved within a Bayesian filter framework. Additionally, the frequency dynamics with uncertain-variability features are reformulated into a set of equivalent chance-constrained constraints, and sequentially integrated into FCUC problems. This approach allows for an efficient examination of the probabilistic characteristics of frequency dynamics induced by uncertain-variability features through the integrated modeling of BF-SFR and r-FCUC. Compared to the authors' previous FCUC work on analytical modeling of frequency dynamics incorporating variable-droop control of converter-based generators, \cite{9765359}, this work presents a novel and fundamentally different modeling framework. This framework treats the frequency dynamic process as a posterior calculation of frequency deviation under complex uncertain-variability environments. By leveraging measurement data to refine and approximate this posterior distribution, BF-SFR ensures that the frequency dynamic trajectory accurately reflects the impact of uncertain-variability feature, even in low-inertia systems.

To summarize, two significant contributions in this work are described below, $i$) A novel BF-SFR model considering uncertain-variability features is constructed, and a associated solution within Bayesian filter theorem is carried out. Through this proposed BF-SFR, both the trajectory and posterior distribution of frequency dynamics can be estimated. $ii$) A new chance-constrained formulation for frequency dynamic constraints is introduced and integrated with the proposed BF-SFR model, leading to the development of a corresponding refined FCUC (r-FCUC).
	
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II introduces the Bayesian-filter-derived SFR model. Section III constructs the chance-constrained frequency dynamic constraints. Section IV extends the formulation of the r-FCUC. Section V verifies the effectiveness of the proposed model using the modified IEEE 6 bus test system and 118 bus test system. Finally, Section VI summarizes the primary findings and conclusions of this work.

\section{frequency dynamic model considering uncertain-variability features}

% This section examines the impact of uncertain-variability features on frequency dynamics and then develops a Bayesian filter-based SFR model. Specifically, it derives the posterior distribution of frequency dynamics using a Bayesian filter and outlines the calculation process using a sequential importance sampling (SIS) algorithm, \cite{yardim2011overview}.

This section examines the impact of uncertain-variability features on frequency dynamics and subsequently develops a BF-SFR model. Specifically, the posterior distribution of frequency dynamics is derived within a Bayesian filter framework, and solved by utlizing a sequential importance sampling (SIS) algorithm. \cite{yardim2011overview}.

Mathematically, the transfer function of SFR concerning a occurred step-type power disturbance $\varDelta p_{{step }}(s)=\varDelta p_{{step }} / s$ can be rewritten as, \cite{9964327,paturet2020stochastic,10098683,9613814},

\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{small}
	\begin{align}\label{eqn:1}
		 & \left(2 H_{G} s+D_{G}\right) \varDelta f(s)=\left({1/s} \varDelta p_{ {step}}\right)-                                                                          \\
		 & \qquad {\left({K_{G}}{ R_{G}^{-1}}\right)\left[ {\left(1+F_{G} T_{G} s\right)}/{\left(1+T_{G} s\right)}\right] \varDelta f(s)}- \notag                            \\
		 & \qquad {\left({K_W R_{W}^{-1}}\right)\left[{(H_W + D_W)}/{(1+T_{W} s)}\right] \varDelta f(s)} - \notag                                                            \\
		 & \qquad {\left({R_{W}^{-1}}\right)\left[{1}/({1+T_{W} s})\right] \varDelta f(s)} - {\left({R_{E}^{-1}}\right)\left[{1}/({1+T_{E} s})\right] \varDelta f(s)} \notag
	\end{align}
\end{small}

The time-domain expression of \eqref{eqn:1} can be transferred by applying the inverse-Laplace-transformation (ILT),

\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{newsubequations}\label{eqn:2}
	\begin{align}
		 & 2H_{\varSigma } \varDelta f^{\prime}(t)  +D_{\varSigma} \varDelta f(t)=\varDelta p(t)  \label{eqn:2a}                                                                  \\
		 & \varDelta p(t) \! =\!\varDelta p_{step}\!-\!\left(\varDelta p_{G}(t)\!+\!\varDelta p_{W,1}(t)\!+\!\varDelta p_{W,2}(t)\!+\! \varDelta p_{E}(t)\right) \!\label{eqn:2b} \\
		 & H_{\varSigma}  =H_{G}+H_{W}, D_{\varSigma}  =D_{G}+D_{W}+{ R_{W}^{-1} } + { R_{E}^{-1}}   \label{eqn:2c}                                                               \\
		 & \varDelta p_{G}(t)   ={K_{G} F_{G}}{ R_{G}^{-1}} \varDelta f(t)- \notag                                                                                                \\
		 & \qquad\quad\quad\,\, {K_G(1-F_G)}{ R_{G}^{-1} }e^{-\frac{t}{T_G}} \!\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\!\!\! e^{\frac{\kappa}{T_G}} \varDelta f^{\prime}(\kappa) d \kappa \label{eqn:2d} \\
		 & \varDelta p_{W_1}(t) =\left(K_{W} {R_{W}^{-1}}\right) \varDelta f(t) \!+\! \left(2H_{W}\! \varDelta f^{\prime}(t) \!+\! D_{W}\varDelta f(t)\right)\label{eqn:2e}       \\
		 & \varDelta p_{W_2}(t)  =\left( { R_{W}^{-1}}\right) \varDelta f(t), \varDelta p_{E}(t)  =\left( { R_{E}^{-1}}\right) \varDelta f(t) \label{eqn:2f}
	\end{align}
\end{newsubequations}

% \begin{figure}[!t] \centering \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth, height = 3.in]{.figures/fig1} \caption{The unified frequency dynamic response of electrical power systems against occurred power
% disturbance}\label{fig1} \end{figure}

\noindent where \eqref{eqn:2a} and \eqref{eqn:2b} define the swing function, \eqref{eqn:2c} denotes aggregated frequency parameters, \eqref{eqn:2d} and \eqref{eqn:2f} define additional rescheduled output of different generators. By substituting $\varDelta f^{\prime}(t) = \nicefrac{[\varDelta f(t)-\varDelta f(t-\delta t)]}{ \delta t}$ into \eqref{eqn:2a}-\eqref{eqn:2d}, the relationships between $\varDelta f(t)$ and $\varDelta p_{G}(t), \varDelta p_{W,1}(t)$ in adjacent time steps can be estimated,

\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{newsubequations}\label{eqn:3}
	\begin{align}
		 & \varDelta f(t+\delta t) = \Bigl [ 0.5{H_{\varSigma}^{-1}}\varDelta p(t)  - \alpha_1\varDelta f(t) \Bigr ] \delta t   \label{eqn:3a}                                                                  \\
		 & \varDelta p_{G}(t)\!=\!{K_{G} }{ R_{G}^{-1}} \varDelta f(t) \!-\! \alpha_2 \!\int_{0}^{t} \!\!\! e^{\frac{t}{T_G}} \!\!\left[\varDelta f(t+\delta t) \!-\! \varDelta f(t) \right] dt  \label{eqn:3b} \\
		 & \varDelta p_{W_1}(t) = \alpha_3 \varDelta f(t + \delta t) - 2H_{W} \delta t \varDelta f(t)  \label{eqn:3c}                                                                                           \\
		 & \alpha_1 = 0.5{D_{\varSigma}}{H_{\varSigma}^{-1}}-1 \label{eqn:3d}                                                                                                                                   \\
		 & \alpha_2 = {K_G(1-F_G)}{ R_{G}^{-1} }e^{-\frac{1}{T_G} t} \label{eqn:3e}                                                                                                                             \\
		 & \alpha_3 = {K_{W} } R_{W}^{-1} \!+\! D_{W} \!+\! 2H_{W} \delta t \label{eqn:3f}
	\end{align} \end{newsubequations}

In \eqref{eqn:2a}-\eqref{eqn:2f}, \(\varDelta f(t\!+\!\delta t)\) is significantly dependent on \(\varDelta p(t)\) while all generators automatically adjust their additional outputs so as to eliminate the imbalanced power disturbance.
Despite that \( \varDelta p_{G}, \varDelta p_{W,1}(t) \) in \eqref{eqn:3b} and \eqref{eqn:3c} can be recursively solved after observing \(\varDelta f(t\!+\! \delta t)\) and \(\varDelta f(t)\), the uncertain-variability feature has not yet introduced, \cite{liu2019model}. To address this, a corrected solution regarding $\varDelta f (t\!+\!\delta t)$ that considers uncertain-variability features is categorized. The uncertain-variability feature contains two parts: $i$) The first component is attributable to constraints imposed on \(\varDelta p_{G}(t),\varDelta p_{W_1},\text{and}\, \varDelta p_{W_2}\), These constraints imply that the additional output produced by generators participating in frequency support may be unattainable due to maximum generation capacity limits. $ii$) The second component is caused by the inherent uncertainty in the output characteristics of converter-interfaced generators, compounded by possible measureable residuals, \cite{9964327,8478399,8122022}.
Let \(\varDelta \omega_{1}(t)\!=\!\!\{\min \) \([\varDelta p_{G}(t), \overline{p}_{G} ] \!+\! \min[ \varDelta p_{W_1}\!(t), {p}_{W_1}(t)] \!+\! \min [\varDelta p_{W_2}\!(t), {p}_{W_2}(t)]\}\) enforces the corrected term for preventing generators from exceeding their maximize outputs, \(\varDelta \omega_{2}(t)=\textrm{Normal}(t; \mu, \sigma)\) denotes the uncertain fluctuations of converter-interfaced generators, where \(\mu\) and \(\sigma\) represents the expected value and variance of uncertain-variability features, respectively. Consequently, the amount of uncertain-variability features can be estimated by summing \(\varDelta \omega_{1}\) and \(\varDelta \omega_{2}\), that is, \(\varDelta \omega\!=\!\varDelta \omega_{1}\!+\!\varDelta \omega_{2}\),

\vspace{-0.125in}

\begin{newsubequations}\label{eqn:4}
	\begin{align}
		\varDelta f(t\!+\!\delta t) \!=\!  \left\{ 0.5 {H_{\varSigma}^{-1}}[\varDelta p(t) \!+\! \varDelta \omega] \!-\! \alpha_1 \varDelta f(t)\right\} \delta t
		\label{eqn:4a}\end{align}
\end{newsubequations}

In \eqref{eqn:4a}, the analytical solution for $\varDelta f(t\!\!+\!\!\delta t)$ may not exist because \(\varDelta \omega\) is governed by a unknown prior distribution. Therefore, the Bayesian filter is applied to address that. Let \(\mathcal{P} (\varDelta f(t))\!,\! \mathcal{P}(\varDelta p(t))\) enforces the posterior of \(\varDelta f(t)\),\(\varDelta p(t)\), respectively, and then, \(\varDelta f(t) \!\!\Rightarrow\!\! \varDelta f(t \!+\! \delta t)\) can be retreated as a hidden decision process, while \(\varDelta p(t)\!\!\Rightarrow\!\! \varDelta p(t\!+\!\delta t)\) be an observable measured process. Consequently, \(\varDelta f(t \!+\! \delta t)\) can be further derived,

\vspace{-0.10in}
\begin{newsubequations}
	\begin{align}
		 & \overbrace{\mathcal{P}(\varDelta f(t+ \delta t)|\varDelta p(t_0\!:\! t \!+\! \delta t); \varDelta \omega)}^{{{predicted\,\, (posterior)}}\,\,function}\!=\!\overbrace{\mathcal{P}(\varDelta p(t\!+\!\delta t) | \varDelta f(t\!+\!\delta t); \!\varDelta\omega)}^{{{{{likelihood}}\,\, function}}} \notag          \\
		 & \underbrace{\mathcal{P}(\varDelta f(t\!+\! \delta t) | \varDelta p(t_0\!:\! t);\! \varDelta \omega)}_{{{{{prior}}\,\, function}}} { {\mathcal{P}^{-1}\!(\varDelta p(t\!+\!\delta t) | \varDelta p(t_0\!:\! t);\varDelta \omega)}}\label{eqn:5}
	\end{align}
\end{newsubequations}
\vspace{-0.125in}

\noindent where the {likelihood} in \eqref{eqn:5} describes the relationship between \(\varDelta p(t\!+\!\delta t)\) and \(\varDelta f(t\!+\!\delta t)\), the prior function describes the sequential recursive process from \(\varDelta f(t_0\!:\! t)\) to \(\varDelta f(t \!+\! \delta t)\). By leveraging the prior and predicted processes within the Bayesian inference framework, the influence of uncertain-variability feature, denoted as \(\varDelta \omega\), can be accurately incorporated. To clearly illustrate the difference between BF-SFR and actual frequency dynamics, the estimation process of frequency dynamics with uncertain-variability features is depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig1}. In particular, the prior function defined in \eqref{eqn:5} can be recursively estimated as follows,

\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{newsubequations}
	\begin{align}
		 & \quad \mathcal{P}\left(\varDelta f(t) \!\mid\!  \varDelta p\left(t_{0}: t+\delta t \right), \varDelta \omega\right) = \label{eqn:6}                                                                                                                     \\
		 & \int\! \bigl[ \mathcal{P}(\varDelta f(t\!+\! \delta t) \!\mid\! \varDelta f(t_0\!:\! t), \varDelta \omega) \mathcal{P}\!\left(\varDelta \!f(t) \!\mid\!  \varDelta p\!\left(t_{0}\!: \! t\right), \varDelta \omega\right) \bigr] d\varDelta f(t) \notag
	\end{align}
\end{newsubequations}


\begin{figure}\vspace{-0.125in}
\centering \includegraphics[width=1.0\linewidth, height = 4.cm]{./.figures/power_system_frequency.pdf}
\caption{The calculation process of frequency dynamics considering uncertain variability-features within a Bayesian filter framework.}
\label{fig1} \end{figure}

Note that both (prior and posterior) functions in \eqref{eqn:5} and \eqref{eqn:6} cannot be directly solved because their distributions are unknown. Therefore, the SIS algorithm is applied to address this. Let \(\mathcal{Q} (\varDelta f(t\!+\!\delta t) | \varDelta p(t_0\!:\!t\!+\!\delta t);\!\varDelta \omega)\) denotes the importance distribution, which serves as a replaceable proposal distribution for generating samples (also called {particles} or {filters}), \(\lambda (\varDelta f(t_0\!\!:\!\!t\!+\!\delta t))\) describes importance weights, which approximate the posterior distribution of the samples according to \eqref{eqn:7a}. Then, the posterior function \eqref{eqn:6} can be indirectly approximated as the product of the importance distribution \eqref{eqn:7b} and the importance weight \eqref{eqn:7b},
\vspace{-0.125in}

\begin{newsubequations}\label{eqn:7}
	\begin{align}
		 & \mathcal{P}(\varDelta f(t)|\varDelta p(t_0\!:\!t+\delta t);\varDelta \omega)=\label{eqn:7a}                                                                                                                                                     \\
		 & \quad \mathcal{Q}(\varDelta f(t)|\varDelta p(t_0\!:\!t+\delta t);\varDelta \omega) \cdot \lambda (\varDelta f(t_0:t+\delta t))\notag                                                                                                            \\
		 & \lambda (\varDelta f(t_0:t+\delta t)) = \frac{\mathcal{P} (\varDelta f(t+\delta t) | \varDelta p(t_0\!:\!t+\delta t);\varDelta \omega)}{\mathcal{Q} (\varDelta f(t+\delta t) | \varDelta p(t_0\!:\!t+\delta t);\varDelta \omega)}\label{eqn:7b} \\
		 & \mathcal{Q} (\varDelta f(t\!+\!\delta t) | \varDelta p(t_0\!:\!t\!+\!\delta t);\varDelta \omega) = \mathcal{Q}(\varDelta f(t\!+\!\delta t)|\varDelta f(t_0\!:\!t); \varDelta \omega)\cdot \notag                                                \\
		 & \mathcal{Q}(\varDelta f(t)|\varDelta p(t_0\!:\!t);\varDelta \omega)  \label{eqn:7c}
	\end{align}
\end{newsubequations}

\section{Frequency constraint reformulation} \vspace{-0.0050in}

This section examines frequency requirements in FCUC studies. On this basis, a revised formulation of frequency nadir restriction based on the BF-SFR considering uncertain-variability features is extended, remaining frequency constraints including RoCoF and FCR can be found in \cite{9765359}.

\vspace{-0.125in}

\subsection{RoCoF constraint}

% \vspace{-0.125in}

The RoCoF constraint aims to mitigate possible rapid changing of \(\varDelta f(t)\) at the IR stage. Mathematically, the differentiation of $\varDelta f^{\prime}(t)$ must not exceed the threshold of change of frequency, denoted as $\gamma_{rocof}$, \textit{i.e.,} $\varDelta f^{\prime}(t)\!\leq \! \gamma_{rocof}$. Since the maximum of $\varDelta f^{\prime}(t)$ equals to ${\varDelta p_{{step} }}/{2 H_{\varSigma}}$, thus, RoCoF constraint can be expressed,

\vspace{-0.125in}

\begin{small}
	\begin{align}\label{eqn:10}
		0.5{H_{\varSigma}^{-1}}{\varDelta p_{{step}}} & \!\leq\! \gamma_{rocof}\Rightarrow H_{G} \!\!+\!\! H_{W}\!\geq\! 0.5 {\varDelta p_{{step} }} \gamma_{rocof}^{-1}
	\end{align}
\end{small}

\subsection{FCR constraint}

The FCR constraint is used to ensure sufficient reserves provided by generators during the PFR stage. the mathematical formulation of the FCR constraint is derived by re-evaluating \eqref{eqn:3a} and \eqref{eqn:3b},

\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{newsubequations}
	\label{eqn:11}
	\begin{align}
		 & \sum\nolimits_{g\in\mathcal{G}} \left( \overline{p}_{g} -p_{g, t}\right) \geq{K_{G} }{ R_{G}^{-1}} \varDelta f(t) \!- \label{eqn:11a}            \\
		 & \qquad \qquad \alpha_2 \!\int_{0}^{t} \!\!\! e^{\frac{t}{T_G}} \!\!\ \varDelta f^{'}(t)dt,t \in (t_{IR}, t_{PFR}]\notag                          \\
		 & \sum\nolimits_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{1}} \left( \overline{p}_{w} - p_{w, t} \right) \geq [K_{W}R_{W_1}^{-1} + D_{W}]\varDelta f(t+\delta t) -  \notag   \\
		 & \qquad\qquad 2H_W \delta t \varDelta f^{'}(t), t \in (t_{IR}, t_{PFR}] \label{eqn:11b}                                                           \\
		 & \sum\nolimits_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{2}} \left( \overline{p}_{w} - p_{w, t} \right) \geq R_{W_2}^{-1} \gamma_{nadir},t \in (t_{IR}, t_{PFR}] \label{eqn:11c} \\
		 & \sum\nolimits_{e\in\mathcal{E}} \left( \overline{p}_{e} -p_{e, t}\right)  \geq R_{E}^{-1} \gamma_{nadir},t \in (t_{IR}, t_{PFR}] \label{eqn:11d}
	\end{align}\end{newsubequations}

% \noindent where \(h_{nadir}\) denotes the maximum frequency deviation. From \(t \!=\! t_{IR}\!\!\rightarrow\!\! t_{PFR}\), \(\varDelta f(t)\) should be kept within the range between \(h_{db}\) and \(h_{nadir}\), and the change of \(\varDelta f^{'}(t)\! \rightarrow\! 0\) at \(t\!=\!t_{PFR}\). Therefore, the binding of the FCR denoted by \eqref{eqn:11a} and \eqref{eqn:11b} can be approximated as,

% In \eqref{eqn:11a} and \eqref{eqn:11b}, \(\varDelta f(t)\) increase while \(\varDelta f^{'}(t)\) decrease during \(t \!\in\! (t_{IR},t_{PFR}]\), and the change of \(\varDelta f^{'}(t)\! \rightarrow\! 0\) at \(t\!=\!t_{PFR}\), therefore, a Conservative but simple of the FCR denoted by \eqref{eqn:11a} and \eqref{eqn:11b} can be approximated as,

As shown in \eqref{eqn:11a} and \eqref{eqn:11b}, the absolute value of \(\varDelta f(t)\) increases while its derivative, \(\varDelta f^{'}\!(t)\), decreases during the time interval \(t \!\in\! (t_{IR},t_{PFR}]\). The derivative approaches zero at \(t\!=\!t_{PFR}\). As such, a conservative albeit simplified approximation of FCR, as represented by \eqref{eqn:12a} and \eqref{eqn:12b}, can be expressed as below,


\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{newsubequations}
	\label{eqn:12}
	\begin{align}
		 &\!\!\!\!\sum\nolimits_{g\in\mathcal{G}} \left( \overline{p}_{g} \!-\! p_{g, t}\right) \!\!\geq \!\! {K_{G} }{ R_{G}^{-1}} \gamma_{nadir}, t \in (t_{IR}, t_{PFR}] \label {eqn:12a} \\
		 &\!\!\!\!\sum\nolimits_{w\in\mathcal{W}_{1}} \!\!\!\!\!\left( \overline{p}_{w} \!-\! p_{w, t} \right) \!\!\geq\!\! (K_{W}R_{W_1}^{-1} \!\!+\!\! D_{W}) \gamma_{nadir}, t \in (t_{IR}, t_{PFR}] \label{eqn:12b}
	\end{align}
\end{newsubequations}

\subsection{Chance-constrained frequency nadir constraint}

The purpose of the frequency nadir constraint is to ensure that \(\varDelta f(t)\) remains within its predefined limit \(\gamma_{nadir}\) at the PFR stage. Given that the posterior distribution of \(\varDelta f(t)\), accounting for uncertain-variability factors, can be accurately determined using \eqref{eqn:5}, \eqref{eqn:6}, \eqref{eqn:7a}, \eqref{eqn:7c}, \eqref{eqn:9a}, and then, a chance-constrained formulation of the frequency nadir constraint can be derived as follows:

\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{small}
	\begin{align}
	& \mathcal{P}\left(\varDelta f(t) \leq \gamma_{nadir}\right) \geq 1-\varepsilon_{SFR},\forall t\in(t_{IR}, t_{PFR}] \label{eqn:13} \\
	& \Rightarrow \,\,\mathcal{P}\left(\vartheta \leq \gamma_{nadir}\right) \geq 1-\varepsilon_{SFR},\notag\\
	& \qquad \vartheta = \max ( |\varDelta f(t)| ),\varDelta f(t) \propto \mathcal{P}(\varDelta f(t)|\varDelta p(t_0:t); \varDelta \omega) \notag
\end{align} \end{small}

% \vspace{-0.125in}
% \begin{small}
% 	\begin{align}\label{eqn:15}
% 	& \mathcal{P}\left(\varDelta f_{\text{nadir}} \leq \varDelta f_{\text{max}}\right) \geq 1-\epsilon
% 	\end{align}
% \end{small}

\noindent where $\varepsilon_{SFR}$ is the confidence coefficient. Using the sampled average approximate (SAA) method that provides a tractable way to handle chance constraints that are otherwise difficult or impossible to solve directly, \cite{yan2021chance}, \eqref{eqn:13} can be reshaped as its deterministic form \eqref{eqn:14}, which is listed below,

\vspace{-0.125in}
	\begin{small} \begin{align}
		\vartheta_{n} - M\tau_{n}\! \leq \!\gamma_{nadir}, (1/{N})\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}  \tau_{n} \leq \varepsilon_{SFR},\forall n \in \mathcal{N} \label{eqn:14}
	\end{align}
\end{small}

\vspace{-0.025in}
\noindent where \(\vartheta_{n}\) represents the realization of \(\max ( |\varDelta f(t)| )\), as derived from its posterior \eqref{eqn:7a}. The terms $n$ and $\mathcal{N}$ denote the index and union set of \(\vartheta_{n}\), while $N$ specifies the total number of sampled instances of \(\vartheta_{n}\). \(\tau_{n}\) denotes a binary variable indicating whether \((\varDelta f(t))_{n}\) satisfying frequency security requirement, $e.g.$, if \(\vartheta_{n}\!\leq\! \gamma_{nadir}\), then \(\tau_{n}\!=\!0\), and vice versa.

% For a preselected mismatch disturbance, \(\varDelta f(t)\) is primarily influenced by aggregated frequency parameters, including inertia, damping, droop, and the fraction of power generated by high-pressure turbines, denoted by \(\langle H_{\varSigma}, D_{\varSigma}, 1/R_{G},F_{G}/R_{G}\rangle\), \cite{paturet2020stochastic,zhang2020modeling,9765359}. As a result, the frequency nadir, which represents the maximum value of \(\varDelta f(t)\) within the interval \(t\in(t_{IR},t_{PFR}]\), can be expressed as a nonlinear combination of these aggregated frequency parameters,


As previously discussed in \eqref{eqn:3a}-\eqref{eqn:3f}, \(\varDelta f(t)\) is inextricably linked to the power imbalance, and this imbalance is influenced by various frequency parameters, including inertia, damping, droop, and the fraction of power generated by high-pressure turbines, denoted by \(H_{\varSigma}, D_{\varSigma}, 1/R_{G}, F_{G}/R_{G} \), \cite{paturet2020stochastic,zhang2020modeling,9765359}. As a result, the value of $\vartheta$ can be represented approximately as a nonlinear function of these aggregated parameters.


\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{small} \begin{align}
	\vartheta =& \max[\phi_{\iota}({\alpha}_{\iota}; \varTheta)],i\in\{1,2,...,5\},\forall \iota\in\mathcal{U} \label{eqn:15} \\
			=&\max\left[\alpha_{\iota,1} H_{\varSigma} \!+\!\alpha_{\iota,2}
D_{\varSigma} \!+\! \alpha_{\iota,3} \left({1}/{R_{G}}\right)\!+\!\alpha_{\iota,4} \left({F_{G}}/{R_{G}}\right)\right.\notag \\
& \left. \alpha_{\iota,5}\right]:H_{\varSigma} \!=\! H_{G} + H_{W}, D_{\varSigma} \!=\! D_{G}+D_{W}+{ R_{W}^{-1} } + { R_{E}^{-1}} \notag
\end{align} \end{small}

\noindent where \(\iota\) and \(\mathcal{U}\) describes the index and set of hyperplanes, defined by function \(\phi_{\iota}(\alpha_{\iota};\varTheta)\). \(\alpha_{\iota}=\cup\{\alpha_{\iota, i}|i\in\{1,2,...,5\}\}\) is the set of fitting parameters, \(\varTheta\) is the set of aggregated frequency parameters, $i.e.$, \(\varTheta=\cup \{H_{\varSigma},D_{\varSigma},F_{G}/R_{G},1/R_{G}\}\). By automatically selecting the closet hyperplane to the \(\vartheta=\max ( |\varDelta f(t)| )\), the value of frequency nadir can be determined. Detailed calculation related to fitting parameters are provided below, \cite{9765359},

\vspace{-0.125in}
\begin{small} \begin{align}
	\min & \sum\nolimits_{j\in\mathcal{J}} \Bigl\{\max[\phi_{\iota}(\alpha_{\iota}; \varTheta_{j})]- \vartheta _{j}\Bigr\} ^{2}, \forall \iota\in\mathcal{U}, j\in\mathcal{J} \label{eqn:16}
\end{align} \end{small}

\noindent where $j$ and $\mathcal{J}$ denote, respectively, the index and union set of all evaluation points sampled from \eqref{eqn:7a}.

% In \eqref{eqn:16}, the inner \(`\max'\) operator identifies the hyperplane closest to each evaluation point, while the outer \(`\min'\) operator ensures global robustness by minimizing the maximum discrepancy between the selected hyperplane and all evaluation points, this hierarchical structure of the problem \eqref{eqn:16} balances the accuracy with global consistency. Mathematically, \eqref{eqn:16} can be solved either by directly employing nonlinear programming solvers such as \code{Ipopt}, \code{Mosek}, etc., or by reformulating it into a recursive problem suitable for Mixed Integer Programming (MIP) solvers, including \code{Gurobi}, \code{IBM CPLEX}, etc. A comprehensive discussion on the derivation and computational strategies for solving \eqref{eqn:16} is provided in \cite{9765359}.

Substituting \eqref{eqn:15} into \eqref{eqn:14}, a convex representation of the frequency nadir constraint can be obtained as follows,

	\begin{small} \begin{align}
		\!\!\!\!\!\!\phi_{\iota}(\alpha_{\iota}; \varTheta) \!\!-\!\! M\tau_{n}\!\! \leq \!\!\gamma_{nadir},({1}/{{N}})\!\!\sum\nolimits_{n\in\mathcal{N}}\!\!\! \tau_{n} \!\!\leq\!\! \varepsilon_{SFR},
		  \forall n \!\!\in\!\! \mathcal{N},\iota\!\in\!\mathcal{U}\label{eqn:17}

\section{Conclusion} 

\vspace{-0.025in}

This paper presents a novel well-designed BF-SFR model for encoding frequency dynamic requirements within r-FCUC. The proposed model offers a flexible framework for incorporating advanced frequency control schemes and stochastic residuals, which effectively capture uncertain-variability features. Furthermore, a convex reformulation of the chance-constrained frequency nadir constraints derived from the BF-SFR is presented. Two case studies are conducted to validate the effectiveness and scalability of the proposed BF-SFR and r-FCUC methodologies.

The key findings are as follows:
$i)$ The frequency trajectory derived using BF-SFR aligns closely with simulated curves, and the posterior of frequency deviations with considering various levels of uncertain-variability features can be effectively visualized through BF-SFR.
$ii)$ Incorporating frequency requirements into UC can increase scheduling costs and the number of online generators. However, the scheduling results from FCUC and r-FCUC significantly enhance system frequency security.
$iii)$ r-FCUC demonstrates superior performance in balancing the participation of converters in frequency support while mitigating the internal effects of excessive uncertain-variability features.
